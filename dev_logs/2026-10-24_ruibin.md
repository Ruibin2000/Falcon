## 3. Technical Details and Decisions

### 3.1. Separate startup and monitoring modes

`connect_ue1.sh` and `connect_ue2.sh` perform one startup/self-check, then wait
without further polling or ping. `--exit-on-success` returns after verification
and leaves the connection up. The separate `reconnect_ue1.sh` and
`reconnect_ue2.sh` continuously check/recover their selected UE. Both modes share
the same role lock to prevent concurrent control of one modem.

Ctrl+C requests selected-role PDU disconnection, host-path cleanup, bearer
deletion, modem disable, and verification. It preserves the other UE and
management Wi-Fi. Both roles must be stopped before updating shared helpers.

Short continuous-recovery commands on the Laptop are:

```bash
# UE1 / FR1 terminal
sudo bash ~/falcon-ue-connect/reconnect_ue1.sh --interval 1 --verbose 2
```

```bash
# UE2 / FR3 terminal
sudo bash ~/falcon-ue-connect/reconnect_ue2.sh --interval 1 --verbose 2
```

Level 1 shows attempt numbers/results, level 2 shows steps, and level 3 includes
full details. The wrappers supply their AT ports and `--auto-cell-lock`.

### 3.2. Radio and PDU policy

| Setting | Accepted default or behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Loop interval | 1 s after a completed check/attempt, plus that round's execution time; not a fixed 1-s cycle. |
| Healthy monitoring | One source-bound and one ordinary ping per completed UE round, together with modem/cell/bearer/host checks. |
| Later radio-failure grace | 10 s of observation before forced recovery preparation; initial serial preparation is immediate. |
| Cell-lock cooldown | 30 s minimum gap before another radio-recovery preparation/request under the documented policy. |
| Background registration | 5-s mmcli client wait, 10-s process watchdog; only one pending request for that UE. |
| PDU disconnect | 2-s mmcli client wait, 3-s process watchdog. |
| CU PDU-request window | 10 s, independent of registration/disconnect client timeouts. |

Client timeout does not prove that the modem or ModemManager backend operation
has finished. Pending operations and transitioning states suppress duplicate
requests while current connection checks continue. A correct saved cell lock is
reused rather than rewritten on every failed attempt. A suitable reported PDU
is preserved during radio search; stale or suspended data paths still follow
the guarded recovery flow.

After a confirmed release or a newly validated allocation, the script reads
the current IP/prefix/interface and rebuilds that role's host path. An unchanged
ModemManager bearer object number does not imply unchanged PDU addressing.
Neither a new IP value nor reuse of the previous value is assumed.

UE1 uses pool `10.45.0.0/16`, service `10.45.0.1`, and table `151`; UE2 uses
`10.46.0.0/16`, `10.46.0.1`, and table `152`. Guards at `20151`/`20152` block
ordinary traffic to those service addresses from falling back through the main
Wi-Fi route when a UE path is absent. Other management destinations retain
Wi-Fi access. The October 23 TUN split remained the baseline, not a new split
performed on October 24.

### 3.3. Timing and final recovery results

Laptop Chrony was configured with `ntp.ubuntu.com` and `time.cloudflare.com`,
its local-reference mode was disabled, and persistent external sources were
saved under `/etc/chrony/sources.d/falcon-standard-time.sources`. The operator
reported synchronization after restart and an approximately 0.127-ms remaining
clock correction. This is not a measurement of absolute clock accuracy.
The final experiment used native UTC timestamps without the historical
199.6-s adjustment; cross-host intervals remain approximate.

The final analyzed window was **18:09:15.370–18:25:39.768 EDT**. UE2 used PCI 2,
NCI `66C001`, SSB ARFCN `639648`, and a 3600-MHz n78 carrier. Both project
paths were physically n78; “FR3” remained the second path's logical label.
DU2 RX gain stayed at its recorded startup value of **35 dB**. Fault injection
used two acknowledged **TX 70 → 40 → 70 dB** cycles.

| Measurement | Cycle 1 | Cycle 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restored gain → first detected PRACH, Spark clock | 22.223 s | 21.076 s |
| PRACH → new PDU resource response, Spark clock | 1.359 s | 1.119 s |
| Restored gain → watcher SUCCESS, approximate cross-host timing | 24.621 s | 23.141 s |
| Successful recovery attempt execution, Laptop clock | 1.556 s | 1.552 s |
| Newly verified allocation | `10.46.0.45/30` | `10.46.0.46/30` |
| Source-bound / ordinary ping RTT | 22.413 / 69.071 ms | 40.793 / 30.460 ms |

Startup attempt 1 succeeded in **3.651 s** with the existing correct lock.
The complete verified IP progression was `.44 → .45 → .46`. Across attempts
1–84, the log recorded three PDU connect commands, zero PDU disconnect commands,
and one startup registration request. The 81 failed retries stopped at serving
cell checks 25 times and registration checks 56 times. Their durations were
0.031–0.734 s before the configured interval; they are correlated observations
during outages, not independent connection-success trials.

There were **637 healthy monitoring rounds**, each with both ping modes passing.
After the second recovery, 576 healthy rounds continued for **789.850 s** until
the last accepted check. The largest measured delay preceded renewed PRACH
detection; the logs do not distinguish UE internal recovery from access attempts
the DU did not detect. They do not establish a receive-gain cause.

Gain timestamps refer to observation of successful console acknowledgments,
not measured RF settling. The final operator-directed closing stage, attempts
85–87 and one terminal health failure, was excluded. DU2 stopped before the UE
shutdown request, so that closing timeout was not counted as failed recovery.
